# Quote

> "\"Graph engineering\" sits on a treadmill of terms. Each one described a real shift. Each one got turned into content slop within weeks:
>
> * 2023: prompt engineering. Craft the words you send the model.
> * Mid-2025: context engineering. Curate everything in the window.
> * June 2026: loop engineering. Design the agent's act-observe-retry cycle.
> * July 2026: graph engineering. Design what happens between and across loops." [^aio]

# Analysis

The most useful single artifact for periodizing the discourse, because it makes a double claim: the names are churn, *and* each names a real migration of the engineering surface — words → window → cycle → topology. Read as a sequence, the object of design expands monotonically: each discipline takes the previous one's whole system as a component. ([Gao Dalie's three-tier version](gd--three-tier-reliability.md) makes the same structure explicit as reliability layers; [Bouchard's rendering](lb--this-weeks-name.md) adds "harness engineering" between context and loop, and shrugs that they "all mostly mean the same" — the two positions are the poles of this bundle's central historiographic question.) The one-line definition of graph engineering here — "design what happens between and across loops" — is arguably the best neutral definition in the corpus: it stays agnostic between the orchestration and knowledge senses while locating the discipline's address precisely.
