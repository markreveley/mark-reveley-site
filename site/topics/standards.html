Normative and informative elements

"3.2.1 normative element: element that describes the scope of the document (3.1.1) or sets out provisions"

"3.2.2 informative element: element intended to assist the understanding or use of the document (3.1.1) or that provides contextual information about its content, background or relationship with other documents"

Clause 3.2, "Elements of a document," in the directive that governs how every ISO and IEC standard is structured and drafted. The distinction is load-bearing: which elements of a standard a claim of conformance must satisfy (normative), and which exist only to help a reader understand or apply it (informative) — including scope, foreword, and notes.

ISO/IEC Directives, Part 2 (8th edition) · 2018

Normative and informative references

"Reference lists must indicate whether each reference is normative or informative, where normative references are essential to implementing or understanding the content of the RFC and informative references provide additional information."

Section 4.8.6, "References Section." Unlike ISO/IEC and W3C, the IETF frames the distinction at the level of a document's references rather than its own body text — a cited RFC is normative if the one citing it cannot be implemented or understood without it, informative otherwise. When a document has both, the RFC Editor requires the reference list itself to be split into two labeled subsections.

RFC 7322, RFC Style Guide (RFC Editor) · September 2014

  • https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc7322.txt

Normative and informative text

"normative: Text in a specification which is prescriptive or contains conformance requirements."

"informative: Text in a specification whose purpose is informational or assistive in the understanding or use of the specification, and which contains no conformance requirements or test assertions."

From the glossary of the W3C Recommendation that guides how W3C specs are written; "informative" is also used interchangeably with "non-normative" across W3C documents. The same normative/informative split organizes conformance across every W3C spec — CSS, HTML, WAI-ARIA, and the rest — even where each spec restates it in its own words.

QA Framework: Specification Guidelines (W3C Recommendation) · 17 August 2005
